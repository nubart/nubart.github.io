3

Run Your First Test

Plan your 30 minutes. You can split them over several sessions and several days. Keep about 10 minutes for a second test after we have worked on your results (step 5). Minutes count while the microphone is unmuted, so mute it whenever nobody is speaking. Unused minutes will expire when you place the order for an event.

Test in a setting as close as possible to the real event: ideally the real speaker reads part of their talk with the same microphone that will be used at the event.

Ask two people on your team to check the result while the speaker talks:

  • Person 1 checks the transcription. On a listener device, they select the same language as the speaker and turn the sound off. The screen now shows what the speech recognition wrote down. They note every word that does not match what the speaker said.
  • Person 2 checks the translation and the voice. On another listener device, with headphones, they select the main language you want to translate into. They note terms that are translated wrongly and names that are pronounced badly.
The listener device starts by default in the language of its browser. Select the language you want manually with the button .

If taking notes is too much work, you can leave it to us: we will check the transcript of your test session.

4

Send Us Your Results

When you consider your first test finished, reply to the email of the Nubart team member who sent you this link. Please send all your notes in one message: your prepared test includes one round of revisions. For each problem, tell us:

  • the word you expected, and what you read or heard instead;
  • whether it was a problem of transcription, translation or pronunciation.
5

Test Again

Our team will work on your list, and we will let you know when the improvements are in place. Then use your remaining minutes to test again, in the same way as described here.

Good to know

Want to test with a recording? Send the sound by cable. A recording played through loudspeakers loses much of its quality, and background music confuses the speech recognition. If you play a video in another browser tab on the speaker device, the browser may consider it an echo and filter it out. If you test with a recording:
  • Use a recording of one person speaking clearly, without music, and with content that matches the material you uploaded.
  • Play it on a separate device and send its sound to the speaker device by cable, ideally through a USB audio adapter with a line input. A plain audio cable between two headphone jacks does not work. Keep the volume of the playing device low. Details are in our instructions.
  • Check that the audio quality indicator is green. If it is not, the test has a problem with the quality of the incoming sound, not with the translation.
Most problems start with the transcription. A name or term can go wrong at three points: when the speech recognition hears it, when it is translated, or when the synthetic voice reads it aloud. In our experience, most errors happen at the first point: if the system writes down the wrong word, it cannot be translated correctly. That is why the transcription check in step 3 is so useful. Read more in our article Why AI Interpretation Still Gets Names Wrong After Preparation.
What preparation can and cannot do. Preparation makes it much more likely that names and terms are translated correctly, but it does not guarantee it. At the real event, the speaker's accent, the sound conditions at the venue and spontaneous speech, such as audience questions, can still cause new errors, as they can with human interpreters. Live AI interpretation cannot be perfect.
