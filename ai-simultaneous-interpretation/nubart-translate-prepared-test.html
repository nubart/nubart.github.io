1

Upload Your Material

Log in and upload your material in the Assets section of your customer area. Send what you would give a human interpreter to prepare for the session:

  • Programme, slides, speaker biographies, product sheets or other texts about the content.
  • The names of the speakers.
  • The languages that will be spoken (not the languages your listeners will choose).
  • Your logo, if we don't have it yet. You can also send it by email.

Give each file a descriptive name. Files must contain text: scanned documents, images, audio and video cannot be read. If necessary, you can also send us a list of terms that should always be translated in a specific way.

We start the preparation once your payment and your material have arrived.
2

Wait for Our Confirmation

Our team usually needs up to 24 hours on working days to prepare the AI. On weekends and public holidays it can take longer.

Please do not start testing before we confirm that everything is ready. Before that, you would be testing the standard system and using up your test minutes.
3

Run Your First Test

Plan your 30 minutes. You can split them over several sessions and several days. Keep about 10 minutes for a second test after we have worked on your results (step 5). Minutes count while the microphone is unmuted, so mute it whenever nobody is speaking. Unused minutes expire when you order an event.

Test in a setting as close as possible to the real event: ideally the real speaker, reading part of the real talk into the microphone that will be used at the event.

Ask two colleagues to check the result while the speaker talks:

  • Colleague 1 checks the transcription. On a listener device, they select the same language as the speaker and turn the sound off. The screen now shows what the speech recognition wrote down. They note every word that does not match what the speaker said.
  • Colleague 2 checks the translation and the voice. On another listener device, with headphones, they select the main translation language. They note terms that are translated wrongly and names that are pronounced badly, and write down how these names should sound.
The listener device starts in the language of its browser. Select the language manually with the button .

If taking notes is too much work, you can leave it to us: we will check the transcript of your test session ourselves.

4

Send Us Your Results

When you consider your first test finished, reply to the email in which you received this page. Please send all your notes in one message: your prepared test includes one round of revisions. For each problem, tell us:

  • the word you expected, and what you read or heard instead;
  • roughly when it was said;
  • whether it was a problem of transcription, translation or pronunciation.
5

Test Again

We work on your list and let you know when the improvements are in place. Then use your remaining minutes to test again, in the same way.

Good to know

Testing with a recording? Send the sound by cable. A recording played through loudspeakers loses much of its quality, and background music confuses the speech recognition. A video played in another browser tab on the speaker device may even be filtered out by the browser as echo. If you test with a recording:
  • Use a recording of one person speaking clearly, without music, and with content that matches the material you uploaded.
  • Play it on a separate device and send its sound to the speaker device by cable, ideally through a USB audio adapter with a line input. A plain audio cable between two headphone jacks does not work. Keep the volume of the playing device low. Details are in the full instructions.
  • Check that the audio quality indicator is green. If it is not, the test shows a problem with the sound, not with the translation.
Most problems start with the transcription. A name or term can go wrong at three points: when the speech recognition hears it, when it is translated, or when the synthetic voice reads it aloud. In our experience, most errors happen at the first point: if the system writes down the wrong word, the translation never sees the right one. That is why the transcription check in step 3 is so useful. Read more in our article Why AI Interpretation Still Gets Names Wrong After Preparation.
What preparation can and cannot do. Preparation makes correct names and terms much more likely, but not certain. At the real event, the speaker's accent, the sound conditions at the venue and spontaneous speech, such as audience questions, can still cause new errors, as they can with human interpreters. Live AI interpretation cannot be perfect.
