1. SHORT ANSWERS TO THE MOST COMMON QUESTIONS

Does Nubart train AI models on my content?
No. Nubart does not use customer content to train AI models, and its technology partners agree by contract not to do so.

Where is my data processed?
Mainly in the European Union (Ireland and Germany). For some less common languages, and for the optional event preparation, a specialized provider outside the EU may be involved. These transfers are covered by Standard Contractual Clauses. Section 5 gives details.

Is the speaker's audio stored?
No. Audio is processed live.

Who can see the documents I upload for event preparation?
Your own admins and the speakers who uploaded them, plus authorized Nubart staff who prepare your event. These files are not deleted automatically. They stay until you delete them. Section 4 explains who can see and delete what.

Is a human involved?
People prepare the event beforehand and review problems afterward. During the event, the translation is fully automatic.

How accurate is it?
Nubart does not give one accuracy figure. Section 6 explains why and what Nubart does to support good quality.

2. WHAT NUBART TRANSLATE IS AND WHAT IT IS NOT

Nubart TRANSLATE is a service for live simultaneous interpretation at events. A speaker talks, and listeners hear the translation in their own language on their own phone or laptop, in a web browser. No human interpreters take part. All translation is done by AI.

Nubart builds and runs the platform. Nubart does not develop its own speech or translation models. The service combines established AI services from specialized providers for three tasks: speech recognition, machine translation and speech output. The providers are listed in Annex III of the DPA.

The service is made for in-person and hybrid events, with one or more speakers and many listeners. It is not meant for high-risk uses such as emergency services, medical decisions or legal proceedings (Terms 7.2, High-Risk Activities, and 9.1.4).

3. HOW A TRANSLATION IS MADE, STEP BY STEP

Each speaker logs in and chooses the language they will speak. The system does not guess the language. This keeps the process predictable. If the audio does not seem to match the chosen language, the speaker's screen shows a warning.

Then three steps run in real time. First, speech recognition turns the speaker's voice into text in the original language. Second, machine translation turns this text into the language of each listener. Third, speech output turns the translated text into spoken audio. Listeners hear an AI voice, not the speaker's own voice. Nubart does not clone voices.

Each step uses a specialized service, and text passes from one step to the next. This is why the process can be described and checked. When something goes wrong, the cause can usually be traced to one of the three steps.

Nubart can explain the system: what each step does, what goes in, what comes out and where the data goes. Nubart cannot explain why an AI model chose one word over another. Current AI translation models do not give a reason for each word they choose. Nubart's explanation is therefore at system level, not at model level.

4. HOW THE AI IS PREPARED FOR EACH EVENT

Before an event, the customer can upload documents such as the programme, slides and a list of speakers to a password-protected area. This step is optional.

A Nubart staff member then starts an automated procedure. It sends the uploaded documents to an AI service, which drafts three things: a short context text, a custom vocabulary for the speech recognition step, and term lists for the translation step. They help the system with the names and technical terms of that event. The AI service receives only the material the customer provided. It does not receive audio, live transcriptions or live translations. The AI provider deletes its own copy within 30 days, except where law or the enforcement of its usage policy requires longer. This period applies only to the provider's copy. The original files stay in the customer's password-protected area until the customer deletes them (see below).

Nubart staff then check the result by hand and correct it where needed. This preparation does not train or change any AI model. It gives the services extra information while they run for this event. It improves the chance that names and terms are right. It cannot guarantee it.

There is a privacy trade-off. The more detailed and human-checked a preparation is, the more staff members read the material. Access is limited to authorized Nubart staff who need it, it is logged and monitored, and staff are bound by confidentiality agreements (Terms 5.5, Access Controls). For confidential material, a customer can upload less, or not use this step at all.

Nubart does not delete uploaded files automatically. A file stays in the customer's password-protected area until someone with the right permission deletes it. Customers can do this as soon as the event is over, or earlier, once Nubart has confirmed that the material was used in the preparation. Customer admins can open and delete all uploaded files. Speakers with "speak and translate" permission can open and delete only their own uploads. Other team members see only the file names and the short explanatory lines that the uploader typed in during the upload, so they know what has already been uploaded, but they cannot open files they did not upload. Nubart cannot restore a deleted file. It is removed from Nubart's main systems at once and from backups within two weeks. The context texts and term lists generated for an event stay with the event setup until the customer asks Nubart in writing to delete them (Terms 5.3.4).

5. HOW DATA IS HANDLED

Audio is processed live and is not stored. For each event, the customer chooses what happens to transcriptions and translations: delete immediately after processing, delete after 30 days, or keep until the customer asks for deletion. Nubart also holds speaker account data (name, email, hashed password), event details such as date, duration and audio quality indicators, and the uploaded preparation files, which stay until the customer deletes them. Listeners are anonymous. They do not log in and give no personal data.

The main processing runs on cloud infrastructure in the European Union (Ireland and Germany). Stored transcriptions and translations are held in a database hosted in Ireland. For a few less common languages, speech output is made by a specialized provider in the USA. It receives only the translated text, with no audio and no speaker or event details. The drafting step described in section 4 may be processed outside the EU, including in the USA. Transfers outside the EU are covered by Standard Contractual Clauses. Annex III of the DPA lists each provider, its location and its safeguards.

Nubart and its providers do not use customer content to train AI models. Data is encrypted in transit (TLS 1.2 or higher) and at rest (AES-256). Nubart informs customers within 72 hours of a confirmed breach, and customers have information and audit rights (DPA sections 6 and 7). If Nubart adds or replaces a provider, it informs customers, who can object within 14 days (DPA 3.3).

For confidential events, customers can use non-transferable access cards instead of the standard QR code. They make unauthorized sharing much harder. No access system is fully immune.

6. QUALITY AND ACCURACY: WHAT TO EXPECT AND HOW NUBART SUPPORTS IT

Nubart does not give one accuracy figure for the service, because a single number would mislead. There are three reasons.

First, the result depends on the conditions. Microphone, room, background noise, the speaker's accent and pace, and the subject change the result a lot.

Second, most errors start in the first step. If speech recognition hears a word wrongly, the translation is a correct translation of the wrong word. For example, a speaker says "revenue will grow by fifteen percent", and the recognition hears "fifty percent". The listener then hears a perfect translation of "fifty percent". The translation step cannot know what was really said.

Third, there is rarely only one correct translation. Several different renderings of a sentence can all be accurate. A percentage based on comparison with one reference text does not capture this. Quality is better judged by counting errors that change the meaning (wrong meaning, omissions, additions, wrong terms) and by how serious they are.

Nubart's public speaker instructions mention an indicative range of 85 to 95 percent under good conditions. This is an estimate based on Nubart's experience, not a measured result. Nubart plans to develop a structured accuracy check.

What Nubart does to support good quality:

  • A free 30-minute trial for first-time users, before they place an order (Terms 3.1.3). Nubart may also offer a paid test with event preparation (Terms 3.1.4, Prepared Test).
  • A spectrum meter to check whether the signal from the microphone is reaching Nubart's system correctly.
  • A real-time audio quality indicator on the speaker's screen. It alerts when quality falls below about 90 percent on Nubart's own metric. This is an audio measure, not an accuracy score. A language mismatch alert works the same way.
  • Event preparation from the customer's documents (section 4).
  • Setup and testing recommendations in the public speaker instructions.

If a customer reports a quality problem and has not chosen immediate deletion, Nubart can produce an internal quality report for the event. It shows the transcription, all translations, and the audio quality for each sentence, with average and median. This shows whether an error began in speech recognition or in translation. There are two limits. Speech output is not recorded, so Nubart cannot check what listeners heard. And because no audio is stored, Nubart cannot verify what the speaker really said. This protects privacy, and it also limits what can be audited.

Nubart does not currently run systematic accuracy measurements across events. Customers who need evidence on their own content can ask Nubart about a test on their event material, with criteria agreed in advance. A suitable approach is a review by a bilingual reviewer who records the audio conditions, separates speech recognition errors from translation errors, and counts meaning errors by type and seriousness.

Known limits: poor audio, background voices, strong accents, very fast speech, mixed languages, specialized terms and names that are not in the preparation, and spontaneous speech such as audience questions, which cannot be prepared. The AI can occasionally produce wrong content, and rarely inappropriate content (Terms 2.5 and 9.1, AI Nature and Output).

7. HUMAN OVERSIGHT, RESPONSIBLE USE AND CHANGES

During the event the translation is fully automatic. People are involved before and after it. Nubart staff review each event preparation. The customer's team tests the setup, briefs the speakers and watches the audio quality indicator. Nubart may provide emergency technical support during confirmed events. After the event, quality complaints are reviewed as described in section 6.

AI interpretation should not be the only basis for high-stakes decisions, such as legal, medical, financial or safety matters (Terms 9.1.4). In such cases, critical information should be checked by qualified people. Nubart recommends that customers tell attendees that the translation is AI-generated and not certified.

Nubart may update or replace AI components. It gives at least 30 days' notice of material changes that may affect quality. If quality drops materially after such a change, the customer can report it to Nubart with examples and ask Nubart to investigate and fix it within 15 business days. If it is not fixed within that time, the customer can end the agreement with 7 days' written notice and without penalty (Terms 2.4).
